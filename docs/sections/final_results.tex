\section{Final Results}

After conducting the final experimental run to identify the best-performing integrator with updated parameters
of a higher-order Leapfrog-4 integrator on par with previous integrators in the comparison table, the following
was discovered.

\subsection{Runtime}

As shown on the Figure 13, we may observe Leapfrog being the fastest performing integrator at higher resolutions.
The result is close to the Velocity Verlet integrator, although the two integrators are mathematically related.
RK4 shows the highest possible runtime due to the fact of it having two more orders.

The updated Leapfrog-4 integrator shows the runtime higher to its predecessor, although this is explained by
the application of higher order Yoshida method.

\subsection{Energy Conservation}

Figure 14 presents the distribution of relative energy errors for each integrator across the three tested
timesteps. Explicit Euler sits consistently around a relative error of $10^0$ regardless of resolution, with a
very narrow spread, indicating that the method fails to conserve energy irrespective of how finely the
timestep is refined. Symplectic Euler, Leapfrog, and Velocity Verlet all show a clear downward trend in
error as the timestep decreases, which is expected of lower-order methods whose local truncation error
scales with $\Delta t$. RK4 displays the widest spread of the five integrators, ranging from below $10^{-12}$
at $\Delta t = 10^{-3}$ up to over $10^1$ at $\Delta t = 10^{-1}$, showing that its performance is highly
sensitive to the choice of timestep.

The Leapfrog-4 integrator shows the lowest spread of the relative energy error distribution at its highest
timestep of $\Delta t = 10^{-3}$ offering higher near-machine precision than previous Leapfrog and RK4.
It is also worth mentioning that due to the higher order of Leapfrog-4, the precision is higher even at lower
resolutions of $\Delta t = 10^{-1}$ and $\Delta t = 10^{-2}$.
